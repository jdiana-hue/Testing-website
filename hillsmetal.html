Class project

Metal Detection Validation (Hill's Pet Nutrition)

Introduction. Metal fragments are among the most serious foreign-object hazards in food manufacturing, and in dry bagged pet food the metal detectors on the packaging line are the last control point before a bag reaches a consumer. Their reliability has to be demonstrated rather than assumed. I led the validation of five metal detectors at a Hill's Pet Nutrition plant, establishing that each one reliably detects metal fragments across the full range of products it runs.

>Methods. I authored the protocol and the report, and built a sampling plan broad enough to cover the variation the detectors actually encounter: over 15 different diets and bag sizes, with a sample size of over 200 per diet. Product density and bag size both affect detector sensitivity, so testing a single product would not have supported any conclusion about the line as a whole. I scheduled the runs directly with the plant manager, assisted in execution, and verified that every run was performed and documented according to the protocol.

Results. All five metal detectors were validated successfully, and no deviations were recorded across any diet or bag size tested. The results were documented in the final validation report, which released the detectors for continued production use.

Discussion The sampling plan was really the whole project. Testing 15 diets across 5 detectors meant the result said something about the line as a whole, not about one convenient setup, and designing that coverage is what makes it a validation instead of a demo. Fitting that much testing around a live production schedule, without stopping the line, was the harder half, and the part that leaned most on the plant team.
