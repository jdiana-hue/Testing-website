PECO Fill-level Inspection Validation (Hill's Pet Nutrition)

Introduction. Canned pet food must be filled to a specified level, both because consumers are paying for a stated quantity and because fill level determines the nutrition an animal actually receives. Hill's Pet Nutrition uses PECO inspection systems, which send a beam through the sealed metal can to confirm fill level before final packaging. Those systems had begun producing an increasing number of false negatives, passing cans that should have been rejected, so a validation was scheduled both as routine assessment and to establish whether the systems remained fit for use. I led that validation as the responsible quality engineer.

Methods. I authored the validation protocol and defined the sampling plan: three different diets, with over 100 cans tested per diet, so that the result covered product variation rather than a single formulation. Testing ran on production-equivalent equipment, so that the conditions matched what the line actually operates under. Execution required coordinating with plant managers to schedule the specific cans and to run the test without interrupting production, and I was present throughout to confirm that each step was performed and documented as written. The record was built in SAP and Kneat.

Results. Across all three diets the systems performed as required, and the validation was completed with no deviations. I compiled the data into the final cGMP validation report, which documented the systems as suitable for continued production use.

Discussion. Passing wasn't really the point, and the record behind it was what mattered, because a system is only validated when the documentation can show someone who wasn't there that it got challenged properly and held up. Building that record in SAP and Kneat taught me that validation is as much a documentation job as a testing one, and I hold computational work to the same standard when I report where a model works and where it doesn't.
